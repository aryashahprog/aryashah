## The claim

Most AI tools assume someone sitting at a desk. People working on a floor, in a field or on a route need different interfaces. AR glasses may be an intermediate step toward robotics doing more of the physical work.

## Reasoning

- Frontline workers often have their hands busy and are moving around. Typing into a chat window doesn't fit.
- Voice and vision through something worn may fit better than a phone or laptop.
- AR as a step in between: a person still does the task but gets guidance as they work. The data from that work might later help train robots.

## Open questions

- Labor constraints: whether difficulty hiring is what actually pushes companies to adopt these tools.
- Synthetic data: whether it can stand in for real-world data when training robots.
- Hardware: whether glasses become comfortable and affordable enough to wear all day.

These are questions, not conclusions.

## Where value might accrue

- Interfaces designed around real frontline workflows.
- Companies that capture useful data about how physical work gets done.

## What would change my mind

- Frontline workers sticking with phones and finding them good enough.
- Robotics progressing without needing an AR step in between.
- Data from glasses turning out not to be useful for robotics.
