A lot of very different AI companies seem to be heading toward the same customer. The enterprise that wants someone to make AI work inside its business.

Data companies, model specialists and deployment teams start in different places. My bet is that more of them end up selling the same thing: a change in how work gets done.

That’s what I mean by AI diffusion. Taking what the models can do and getting it into the parts of a company where people actually use it.

## Different starting points. A similar destination.

Businesses across data, post-training and inference can move toward enterprise AI transformation. Selling to a few labs can fund the move into a much broader customer base.

I think the direction makes sense. A data business can build relationships, credibility and cash flow that help it reach enterprise buyers. But having those things doesn’t automatically make it good at changing an enterprise workflow. That is a second business it still has to build.

The destination might look similar. The work required to get there can be very different.

## The hard part lives inside the workflow

Take a finance team closing the books. Connecting a model to its documents is one step. Getting it to understand why a particular adjustment happens every quarter is another. So is knowing who can approve it and what evidence the auditor needs.

The spreadsheet captures part of that. The person who has been doing the job for twelve years holds the rest.

**As execution gets easier, the unwritten rules become more valuable.** The exceptions. The judgment calls. The reason a process looks inefficient but exists anyway.

A useful product has to capture that knowledge, keep it current and let people check what the system did. Then someone has to get the team comfortable enough to use it. Training and change management are part of delivering the outcome.

## Diffusion happens one workflow at a time

I’ve been thinking about this through three questions. How much unwritten knowledge does the task need? How sensitive is the data? How expensive is it to deploy and run reliably?

A routine task using public information may work well in a general assistant. A financial process involving private records, recurring exceptions and expensive mistakes may need deeper integration, persistent memory and tighter controls.

That doesn’t settle cloud versus private deployment. It gives you a better place to start than assuming every department needs the same AI stack.

It also explains why a horizontal model getting better doesn’t settle the application question. Better capabilities can make more workflows possible while leaving plenty of work to connect them to a company’s systems and way of operating.

## What I’d look for

I’d start with a team that owns one costly workflow and can show that it improves. Less review time. Fewer exceptions sent back to a person. A faster close without sacrificing accuracy.

The question is whether each deployment makes the next one easier. Do the integrations, checks and accumulated workflow knowledge carry over? Or does every customer need a new team rebuilding everything?

Services can get a company close to the problem. The opportunity is turning what it learns there into something repeatable.

I’d rethink this if general assistants start handling these workflows with little setup, or if deployment businesses keep adding people at the same rate as revenue. Being close to the customer matters. Building a business that gets better at serving them matters more.

**The next wave is getting intelligence to work inside the business. That’s the diffusion opportunity.**
