## The claim

Consumer agents can act on someone's behalf without ever opening the app or website. If that grows, websites, customer service lines and SaaS products may need to treat agents as users.

## Reasoning

- Much of the web is designed for a person looking at a screen: layouts, ads, upsells, onboarding flows.
- An agent comparing, booking or buying cares about different things: clear information, clear prices and a reliable way to complete the action.
- Businesses that depend on people seeing their interface, through ads or upsells, would feel this first.
- Businesses that are easy for agents to work with might get more requests sent their way.
- The same logic could reach service lines and support, if agents start contacting businesses for people.

## Where value might accrue

- Making products and services easy for agents to understand and use.
- Payments and identity for purchases an agent makes on someone's behalf.
- Businesses that win on the product itself rather than on their interface.

## What would change my mind

- People not trusting agents to act for them, especially with money.
- Platforms blocking agent access to their services.
- Agent use staying niche.
