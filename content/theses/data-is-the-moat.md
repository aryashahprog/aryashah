## The claim

The title is a hypothesis, not a finding. If strong models are widely available, a company's advantage may come from proprietary data and how well it adapts models to that data, rather than from the model itself.

## Reasoning

- If two companies use the same model, what separates them is what they feed it and how they connect it to their work.
- Data a competitor can't buy or collect is harder to copy than a model choice.
- Adapting a model to a company's data (through tuning, retrieval or other methods) is work that builds up over time.

## Counterarguments

- A lot of corporate data is messy, siloed or not that different from a competitor's. Having data isn't the same as having useful data.
- Better general models may need less company-specific data to do well, which would shrink the advantage.
- A data advantage only counts if the company can actually put it to work. Many can't yet.

## Open question

Do large companies build or adapt their own models, or do they buy a new infrastructure layer that does it for them? I don't know yet, and the answer probably varies by company size and industry.

## Where value might accrue

- Companies with unusual data that is hard to collect.
- Whoever helps companies turn their data into something agents can use safely: cleaning it, managing permissions and adapting models to it.

## What would change my mind

- General models consistently matching adapted ones on company-specific tasks.
- Companies with more or better data not getting better results from AI.
