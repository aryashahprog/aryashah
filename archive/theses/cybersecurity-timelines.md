## The claim

My hypothesis is that cheaper intelligence expands what defenders can do and also gives more attackers access to skills they didn't have, and that the net effect is a shorter window to respond to threats.

## Reasoning

- Work that used to take a skilled person, like finding a weakness or writing a convincing phishing message, gets easier when models can help.
- Defenders get the same help: faster triage, code review and patching.
- The two sides aren't symmetric. An attacker needs one way in; a defender has to cover everything.
- If both sides speed up, the time between a weakness becoming known and being used should shrink. I don't have numbers on how much. That needs real data, not assumptions.

## Where value might accrue

- Detection and response that can act at machine speed.
- Fixing problems, not only finding them.
- Security for the agents companies deploy.

## What would change my mind

- Public data showing the time from disclosure to exploitation isn't shrinking.
- Defensive tools gaining clearly more from AI than attackers do.
