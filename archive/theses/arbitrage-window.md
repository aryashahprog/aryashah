## The claim

The cost of building software is falling fast enough that individuals can build a tool at the moment they need it instead of buying one. For now that's an arbitrage window for people who can build quickly. The bigger question is what still differentiates a business when building is cheap for everyone.

## Reasoning

- If someone can describe a tool and have a working version the same day, the bar for "worth building myself" drops a lot.
- Simple, single-purpose software seems most exposed, since it's the easiest to rebuild.
- The window exists because the tools are new and unevenly used. As more people use them, the advantage of simply being able to build shrinks.

## What might still differentiate a business

- Distribution: being where the customers already are.
- Trust, and taking responsibility when something breaks.
- Proprietary data that a rebuilt tool wouldn't have.
- Doing the unglamorous parts well: support, compliance, uptime.
- Knowing which problem is worth solving in the first place.

## Where value might accrue

- People and small teams who use the window to build something useful before the market catches up.
- Businesses whose value comes from the items above rather than from the code itself.

## What would change my mind

- Self-built tools proving costly enough to maintain that people go back to buying.
- Building costs stopping their decline, or quality problems keeping most self-built tools from being usable.
