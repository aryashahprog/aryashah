## The claim

My hypothesis is that AI follows a path like cloud computing: the base layer (models and the infrastructure that runs them) becomes closer to a commodity, and more of the lasting value sits in workflow and vertical applications built on top.

## Reasoning

- My rough read of cloud is that raw compute became something you rent, and a lot of durable businesses were built on top of it. I want to check that read more carefully before leaning on it.
- If models become interchangeable for many everyday tasks, competition at that layer tends to come down to price.
- In that world, what keeps a customer is the workflow: the data, integrations and habits built around a product, not which model sits underneath.

## Where the analogy might break

- Early cloud sold fairly standard compute. Frontier models still differ a lot in what they can do, so the base layer may not commoditize evenly.
- The companies building models also build applications, so they may not stay "just infrastructure."
- Neo-clouds (companies renting out GPU capacity) look like early cloud on the surface. I'm not sure the economics match. Supply of chips and a small set of large customers may matter more for them than they did for early cloud providers. That's a question to check, not a conclusion.

## Where value might accrue

- Vertical applications that own a specific workflow and the data that comes with it.
- Whatever makes it easy for a company to switch between models, if switching becomes routine.

## What would change my mind

- One model keeping a large capability lead for years and charging for it.
- Model providers winning most of the application layer themselves.
- Switching models turning out to be expensive enough in practice that customers stay put.
