## The claim

Agents need more than access to systems. They need the knowledge, expertise and norms of the place they work. Context matters to buyers, but I'm not convinced standalone "context" startups can defend that value.

## Reasoning

- A new hire with full system access still makes mistakes in the first week because they don't know how things are done yet. Agents have the same problem, without the chance to pick it up over lunch.
- So buyers should care about context: it's where many agent mistakes would come from.
- But context mostly lives inside systems companies already use: documents, tickets, CRM, chat. The companies that own those systems, and the model providers, have good reasons to offer context as a feature.
- That makes a standalone context layer hard to defend, even if the problem is real.

## Where value might accrue

- Incumbent platforms that already hold a company's context.
- Vertical tools in fields where the norms are specialized and hard to write down.

## What would change my mind

- A standalone context product becoming something buyers can't easily remove.
- Incumbents failing to share context across each other's tools, leaving room for a neutral layer.

This note is about what agents need to know. [Agent governance](/theses/agent-governance/) is about rules for what they do, and [agent system of record](/theses/agent-system-of-record/) is about the record of what they were allowed to touch and why facts changed.
